TRAVEL PLANNER · NEW FEATURE DESIGN · 4 SEP 2026
Empty states, warning strips, and the Review screen
Three artboards drawn against the existing app, not over it. Every value here is a value already in web/css/app.css — same bone page, same white cards, same four semantic colours, same button ladder, same 16px page sides, same 390 × 844 frame. Nothing existing is redesigned and no new hue, gradient or illustration is introduced. Where a genuinely new decision was needed it is called out as one.
390 × 844 · LIGHT ONLY ARTBOARD 1 · EMPTY STATES ARTBOARD 2 · WARNING STRIPS ARTBOARD 3 · REVIEW Full write-up: docs/design/new-feature-design.md
00 · BEFORE DRAWING
What each artboard actually has to solve
1 · EMPTY STATES
Twelve places are empty for four different reasons. Today they all sound the same.
The app already has one good instinct — empty copy teaches the data model ("the gaps between them become free time by themselves"). What it does not have is a rule for whose emptiness it is. A day you have not planned, a log that has not happened yet, and a day the trip owner has not filled in are three different situations wearing the same grey sentence and, worse, the same button.
Decision to make: what carries ownership — the words, or the surface?
2 · WARNING STRIPS
The warning is a generated English sentence with a proper noun dropped into the middle of it.
Live strings from store.js: "Starts 13:45 but sits after Nishi Market at 13:30." and "Nishi Market runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other." Both are fine until the name is 東京都庁舎第一本庁舎展望室. Then the sentence has no capital letters to find the noun by, no spaces to break on, and 11.5px is below the CJK legibility floor.
Decision to make: keep the name inline in an English clause, or take it out of the grammar altogether.
3 · REVIEW
The behaviour is right. The screen just does not rank its four questions.
Today review.js renders a flat list of identical cards, each with a badge, a title, a two-box .sides comparison and two buttons. Nothing is grouped, an addition renders an empty "YOURS" box, long values squash into 12px columns roughly 150px wide, and the take-all/keep-all pair scrolls away below five cards.
Decision to make: visual ranking of what changed → what I have → what they propose → what I decide, with no change to the mutations underneath.
ARTBOARD 1
The empty-state grammar
One system, three tiers, and a single question that sorts every empty place in the app into one of them: whose turn is it? The answer is carried by the surface, not by the wording — because the wording is the first thing that gets rewritten and the last thing anyone reads.
TIER 1 · YOURS TO FILL
The container exists and you are the one who fills it. Nothing has gone wrong; there is simply a next move.
Surface bone page, no card. Mark a dashed silhouette of the thing that is missing. Copy one 15px line naming what is missing, one 12.5px sentence teaching the model. Action one ink primary, at most one ghost alternative.
Trips home · a day of your own trip · shopping list · a place's tabs
TIER 2 · FILLS ITSELF
Empty because the trip has not happened yet. Time fills this, not a button. Pushing an action here invents a chore.
Surface the existing inline .empty block, 28px/16px, centred, 12.5px soft. Mark none. Copy one sentence, present tense, naming what will fill it. Action no ink button. A dashed add only where doing it early is legitimate.
Log before the trip · spend report before anything is bought · a place's must-see list
TIER 3 · SOMEBODY ELSE'S SIDE
Empty because it is the shared copy and the person who keeps it has not filled it in. You cannot finish this, and you are not being told off about it.
Surface a jade card — jade already means "given to you", and this is the given copy. Mark none. Copy what you were sent, then when more arrives. Action never an ink primary. One ghost, and only for something that is yours to do meanwhile.
A joined trip before its first update · an unplanned day in someone else's copy
THE SIX QUESTIONS, ANSWERED ONCE
What is empty?Name the missing thing, never the container. "No trips yet", not "This list is empty".
Whose emptiness?You (tier 1) · the calendar (tier 2) · the person who keeps the shared copy (tier 3).
Is an action expected?Only in tier 1. Tier 2 waits for a day to happen; tier 3 waits for a person.
Should one be offered?Only if it can be completed right now with what is already on the phone. Otherwise it is a dead end dressed as help.
What action?The same action that already exists elsewhere on that screen, with the same label. An empty state never introduces a control.
When none?Emptiness caused by time, or by another person. Both then get a second sentence saying what will end it.
COPY RULES · SHARED STATE WITHOUT BLAME
DO NOT
"Ana hasn't planned this day yet." · "Waiting for the trip owner." · "No data received."
A person as the subject of a negative verb reads as a complaint about them. "Received" is machine language.
DO
"Day 4 is empty in the copy you were sent." · "Anything Ana adds arrives with the next update."
The copy is the subject of the first sentence, so nobody is at fault. The person appears only in the second, attached to a positive verb — what they will do, not what they have not done. Lower-case after the first word, no exclamation, state the consequence: the house voice, unchanged.

ARTBOARD 2
Warning strips in three scripts
The strip itself is not in question — amber surface, 10.5/800 label, 11.5px text, a fix button that names the tap, sitting on the row it concerns. What is in question is the sentence: the app generates English prose and injects a place name into the middle of it, and a place name is the one part of this product guaranteed not to be English.
THE TEST BENCH · THREE STRUCTURES × THREE SCRIPTS, AT THE REAL 290px STRIP WIDTH
REJECTEDS1 · inline, as built today
The name is a noun phrase inside an English clause.
OVERLAPS
Ashgate Shrine runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other.
OVERLAPS
龍山寺 runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other.
OVERLAPS
東京都庁舎第一本庁舎展望室 runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other.
Fails on three counts. There is no capitalisation in CJK, so the proper noun has nothing to distinguish it from the surrounding words — the eye cannot find which stop is being talked about. The name has no spaces, so the browser breaks inside it, splitting a single word across two lines. And once the name is long the English verb ("runs to") is pushed so far from its subject that the sentence stops parsing.
REJECTEDS2 · inline, name emphasised
Same sentence; the name set in 700 ink and protected from breaking.
OVERLAPS
Ashgate Shrine runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other.
OVERLAPS
龍山寺 runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other.
OVERLAPS
東京都庁舎第一本庁舎展望室 runs to 14:30, so these two are on top of each other.
Better, and still wrong. Emphasis solves findability, and white-space:nowrap stops the name splitting — but only by forcing an early line break, so a long name leaves a half-empty line above it (visible in the third strip). Worse, protecting one name makes the strip's height depend on the length of a place name, which is exactly the value nobody controls. Two strips on the same card then have different heights for no reason the user can see.
CHOSENS3 · the name is lifted out of the grammar
A relation word, the name as a value, the time on the right; the sentence underneath refers to it as "it".
OVERLAPS
WITHAshgate Shrineto 14:30
These two are on top of each other.
OVERLAPS
WITH龍山寺to 14:30
These two are on top of each other.
OVERLAPS
WITH東京都庁舎第一本庁舎展望室to 14:30
These two are on top of each other.
Works in all three, and reads better in English too. The name is no longer a word in a sentence — it is a labelled value, and labelled values are script-neutral. The gap between WITH and the name is a flex gap:8px, not a text space, so the Latin/CJK boundary is spaced by layout rather than by a character the browser sizes wrong. The time is its own flex child on the right: always Public Sans, always tabular, never dragged into the CJK run and never wrapped away from the name it belongs to. Long names wrap inside their own column, indented by nothing and aligned with themselves.
CJK TYPOGRAPHY FINDINGS
Public Sans has no CJK glyphs. Every CJK name in this app is already being rendered by whatever the system falls back to. Make that stack explicit rather than accidental: 'Public Sans','Hiragino Sans','Hiragino Kaku Gothic ProN','Yu Gothic','PingFang SC','Microsoft YaHei','Noto Sans CJK JP',system-ui. Latin characters keep coming from Public Sans because it is first.
CJK reads larger at the same px, and needs more. A 11.5px Han character is optically bigger than 11.5px Latin but far harder to resolve — the strokes, not the box, are the limit. The name therefore moves to 13px and its own line, where it is the only thing at that size. Body copy stays at 11.5px because it is Latin.
Line-height must go up, not stay. CJK glyphs fill their em box, so 1.4 looks cramped. The name line uses 1.35 on one line and up to 1.45 when it wraps; the Latin sentence keeps the existing 1.4.
Never italicise, never letter-space. There is no true italic in any CJK fallback, so emphasis would be a synthesised skew. Weight and position carry emphasis instead — which is already the app's rule.
Punctuation is the giveaway. A pasted name often carries full-width characters (・（）／) with a full em of built-in side bearing. Because the name now sits alone in its column, that padding lands in empty space instead of colliding with an English preposition.
Mixed Latin + CJK is the common case, not the exotic one. "AEON MALL 幕張新都心" is one name in two scripts; the boundary inside it needs no help, because both halves are in the same 13/700 line.
THE STRUCTURE, AND WHAT WRAPS
line 1 · kind10.5/800/.06em amber-fg. Unchanged from today: OVERLAPS, OUT OF ORDER, NO TIME, ENDS WHEN IT STARTS.
line 2 · subjectA flex row, gap:8px, baseline-aligned. Relation word 10/800 in #B99A5E (amber at eyebrow strength) · name 13/700 ink, flex:1;min-width:0 · time 11.5/700 amber-fg, flex:none, tabular.
line 3 · consequence11.5/1.4 amber-fg. Refers to the subject as "it" or "these two" — never repeats the name, so the sentence is short enough never to reach a third line.
line 4 · fixesExisting .warn-fix row, h30, first one ink-filled. Untouched.
wrappingThe name column is the only thing that wraps, and it wraps within itself: line-break:strict so a CJK line never starts with 。、）; overflow-wrap:anywhere so a 30-character name cannot push the time off the strip. Two lines is the practical cap on a 290px strip; beyond that the strip simply grows, which is correct — a warning must never truncate the thing it is warning about.
what did not changeSurface #FBF1DE, r11, 9/10 padding, position on the row, the fix-names-the-tap rule, and the amber semantic. This is the same component with its middle line restructured — not a new notification.

ARTBOARD 3
Review — one row per difference
The behaviour in review.js is unchanged: nothing happens to your day until you press a button on a row, a decided row leaves the list, whatever you skip is not asked again, and take-all / keep-all finish the update either way. What changes is the ranking. The screen has to answer four questions in order — what changed · what do I have · what are they proposing · what am I deciding — and today all four are set at roughly the same weight.
THE FOUR QUESTIONS, RANKED BY WEIGHT
1stWhat changed — the row title, 13.5/700 ink, the heaviest thing on the card. It names the subject (the stop, the item, the day), never the verb.
2ndWhat kind of change — the existing badge, moved to sit alongside a day eyebrow so a reader can scan the kinds down the left edge: THEY ADDED THEY CHANGED THEY REMOVED
3rdYours vs theirs — the existing .sides pair, kept exactly: yours is the quiet box, theirs is jade because jade already means "the copy you were given". Not red-vs-green: nothing here is wrong.
4thThe decision — ghost "Keep mine" and jade "Take theirs", h38, equal width. Deliberately the lightest thing on the card: both answers are fine, and the app never pre-selects one.
Why jade for theirs and not amber. Amber means "yours or guessed" everywhere else in the app; using it for the incoming side would invert the product's one hard rule. Jade means given — and an update is the given copy arriving again. The existing .side.theirs already does this; it is kept.
WHAT IS NEW, AND WHY
Grouped by day, under the existing eyebrow. Differences arrive as a flat array; a person reads them as "what happened to Wednesday". Grouping is presentation only — the entries, their ids and their order within a day are untouched.
Progress in the push sub. "5 things · 2 decided" replaces the bare count, so the header does the counting the disappearing rows imply.
An absent side is written, not blank. An addition has no "yours". Today that renders an empty box; here it reads not on your day in faint — the box keeps its shape so the two sides still align.
Sides stack when either value is long. Above roughly 60 characters the two boxes go full-width, one above the other, so a sentence is never set in a 150px column. Same boxes, same colours, one axis turned.
A delta chip for times. When both sides are clock values the difference is shown as an amber chip (45 min later) so the reader does not do arithmetic on two 12px numbers.
The bulk actions become a sticky foot. .review-foot already exists in the stylesheet (fixed, 1px top line) and is already what the audit describes; the current screen puts the two buttons inline at the end of the scroll, where five cards hide them.
Explanation moves to the foot's fine print. The three-sentence preamble now sits as one line under the sticky buttons — the same words, out of the way of the first row.
