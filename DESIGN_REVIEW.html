What the Travel Planner still needs

The app is deployed and every screen from the turn-2 design is built. What follows is the honest list of where it falls short of a trip you could actually take, split by whether it needs a design decision or just code.

Part 1 — Needs a design decision

Numbered so you can refer to them in the design session. Each one ends with the specific thing I need decided or drawn.

01
Blocker

There is no way to start a trip

The app can only ever show the one demo trip that seeds itself on first launch — Meridian City, day 3. There is no "new trip", no trip list, and no way to edit the trip's name, dates, hotel or currency. Turn 1 of the design had a Trips screen (artboard 1j); turn 2 dropped it and nothing replaced it.

More importantly, your real trips don't start empty — they start as an agent itinerary in a PDF, a WhatsApp message or a photo. Typing six days of stops by hand on a phone is the difference between using this app and not.

To design The Trips list, and a "new trip" flow whose first choice is import: paste text, upload a PDF, or photograph the itinerary. Show what the app does when the import is only half-understood — which fields it asks you to confirm.
02
Blocker

Every screen was designed full; none was designed empty

Days 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 have no stops, so the Map and Plan screens are genuinely blank today. The same is true of a trip with no shopping items, no must-see spots and no notes. I improvised placeholder text, but improvised empty states are how an app starts feeling unfinished.

To design The resting state of each of the ten screens with nothing in it — what it says, and the one action it offers. The empty Plan day is the important one.
03
Blocker

Nothing can be deleted

Only itinerary stops can be removed, and they go to the archive rather than away. Shopping items, packing items, packing categories, places you added, notes, photos and sub-route picks are all permanent once created. One mistyped item stays on the list for the whole trip.

To design A delete affordance that suits a phone (swipe, or long-press, or an edit mode) and a decision per list: archive like the itinerary does, or delete outright. If delete, then an undo — a confirm dialog for every tap gets old fast.
04
Serious

A day can only hold one note, and place notes overwrite it

Notes are stored one per day. Tapping "Add a note" on Nishi Market opens the same composer as tapping Day 3 in the Log, so writing about the market overwrites whatever you wrote about the shrine that morning. The design implies per-stop notes and the storage implies per-day; they contradict each other.

To design Which it is. My recommendation: notes belong to a stop, and the Log day card shows the day's notes stacked with the stop name on each — that matches "log what happened at this stop" and still reads as a diary.
05
Serious

Free time happens more than once a day

The model allows exactly one sub route per day, hanging off one stop. Your own brief describes free time at Shinjuku crossing and elsewhere; a real coach day often has an hour at a shrine in the morning and two hours at a market after lunch. Today the second one has nowhere to live.

To design How the Plan shows two or three separate loops in one day, and how the Sub route screen makes clear which stop you are looping from.
06
Serious

Times are text, and nothing recalculates

In edit mode each time is a free-text box. Nothing derives from it: the "2h15" duration labels are fixed strings, and the coach deadline the buffer counts against is a stored number, so changing Nishi Market's window from 15:45 to 15:00 leaves the buffer claiming you have 37 minutes spare when you don't. Dragging a stop can also put 09:15 after 18:00 and the app says nothing.

To design Whether times are pickers rather than text; how a stop shows its duration as something derived; and what an overlap or an out-of-order day looks like — a warning on the row, or a refusal.
07
Serious

Nobody has decided where place information comes from

The destination screen shows a confident table — opening hours, phone, website, tickets, transport, payment, facilities, language. For the demo stop that content is typed into the seed data. For a real market in a real city, someone or something has to supply it. A places API can, but Google Places needs a billing account and charges per lookup; the free alternatives are thinner.

To design Which fields are worth typing yourself and which must come from a lookup, and what a half-filled table looks like — because most places will be half-filled. This also decides how "+ Add a place myself" behaves.
08
Serious

Must-see spots can be ticked, but not added or photographed

You can mark one of the four seeded shots as taken, and that is all. You cannot add a spot you read about, attach the reference image that made you want it, or attach the photo you actually got. The screen's whole promise — "so I know what must-see when I reach the destination" — depends on content that has no way in.

To design Adding a spot (name, where to stand, best time), attaching a reference image, and what the card looks like once your own photo sits next to the reference.
09
Serious

The app knows you'll miss the coach and never tells you

The buffer exists precisely because missing the bus is the real risk, and it sits silently on a screen you have to open. Nothing notifies you. The same goes for the 14:00 knife-sharpening demo, stalls that shut at 17:00, and the "last chance" airport items on day 6.

To design Where a reminder is switched on (per stop? per shopping item? one setting?) and what it looks like when it fires. Worth knowing: a web app installed to the home screen can send notifications on iOS 16.4+, so this is buildable.
10
Serious

The shopping list can't be corrected or reorganised

Once an item exists you cannot rename it, change its estimate, move it to a different shop, set a quantity, or reorder it. You also asked in your original thirteen changes for grouping by day, and the answer at the time was that the list already sorted that way — it doesn't. It groups by place only.

To design The edit state of an item, and whether "by place" and "by day" are two views of one list or a single grouping with day headers inside each place.
11
Serious

You cannot travel with anyone

Sign-in is anonymous, which means the trip belongs to one browser. Your phone and your laptop hold two unrelated trips, clearing Safari's data starts a third, and there is no way for a partner to tick off the packing list or add to the shopping list. For a family trip that is a real limit, not a technicality.

To design Sign-in (Apple and Google are the natural two), and sharing a trip: who can edit, what an invite looks like, and whether a shared list shows who ticked what.
12
Serious

Bilingual was deferred, and every new screen widens the gap

You chose English-only for this build, which was the right call for speed. But the turn-2 design carried 中文 throughout, and every screen designed from here without Chinese strings is a screen that has to be revisited twice.

To design Keep both languages in the artboards for anything new, and settle the awkward cases now: place names that stay in the local language, and how a long Chinese label behaves in a chip sized for English.
13
Polish

No dark mode

The palette is light only. Half the time you'll open this app is on a coach at night or in bed planning tomorrow, and a full-white screen at 22:00 is unkind. The jade and amber route colours also need checking on a dark ground — amber on near-black loses the "this is mine" contrast it has on bone.

To design A dark palette for the existing tokens, with the route colours re-checked rather than inverted.
14
Polish

Nothing is searchable

Fourteen nearby places and eight shopping items browse fine. A six-day trip with a hundred saved places, four days of notes and a long packing list does not. There is no search on any screen.

To design Whether search is per-screen or one trip-wide search, and what it returns — places, items, notes and shots in one list, or scoped tabs.
15
Polish

The trip recap is promised but doesn't exist

The Log ends with a black card describing a recap — route map, all photos, what you bought, the places you saved but never reached, carried into the next trip. It is the most appealing idea in the whole design and it is a paragraph, not a screen.

To design The recap itself, and specifically the "saved but never reached" list, since that is what makes the next trip start warm.
16
Polish

The design hasn't had an accessibility pass

Some real specifics: the amber-on-cream chips sit near the edge of readable contrast, several labels are set at 9.5–10px, and a few tap targets — the payment chip, the packed-in chip, the sort button — are under the 44px Apple asks for. None of it is fatal; all of it matters on a bright street with cold hands.

To design A pass at minimum sizes and contrast, and a check that the screens survive iOS's larger text settings.

Part 2 — I can fix these in code

No design input needed. These are things I built incompletely or wired to placeholder data, and I'd rather name them than let you find them.

17
Bug

Nearby shows the same fourteen places at every stop

Places are anchored to a stop, but when a stop has no places of its own the code falls back to showing all of them. So opening Nearby from Lumen Crossing lists the places around Nishi Market, half an hour away, with their walking times presented as if they were from where you stand.

18
Gap

The weather is fake

Live forecast was your change request number ten, and the six-day strip, the day pills and the outfit advice all read from seeded numbers that never change. The native app has a working Open-Meteo service; the web app never got it. It needs no key and no billing.

19
Bug

Places you add yourself have no location

"+ Add a place myself" saves a name, category and walking time but no coordinates, so the place never appears on the map and is silently dropped from the "send walk to Maps" route. It should geocode the name, or let you drop a pin.

20
Gap

Offline editing doesn't work, which is the wrong way round

The screens are cached so the app opens without signal, but Firestore's offline cache was never switched on, so reads and writes fail until you're back on data. Free time in a foreign market is exactly when you have no signal — this is a one-line fix and the native app already does it.

21
Bug

The sub-route deadline is a hardcoded number

The buffer counts against 15:45 because that value sits in the seed data, not because it read the coach time off your itinerary. Change the stop, and the buffer keeps quoting the old departure. It should derive from the anchor stop's window.

22
Bug

Day 2's log claims 24 photos it doesn't have

The seeded entry carries a photo count with no photos behind it, so the card renders three empty grey tiles and a "+21". It looks like the images failed to load.

23
Gap

Payment method changes by tapping the chip repeatedly

To set an item to e-wallet you tap the chip three times and watch the label cycle Cash → Card → IC card → E-wallet. It works, it was in the prototype, and nobody would guess it. A small picker would cost nothing.

24
Gap

Currency is fixed at yen, converted at a stored rate

Prices are ¥ and the ≈ RM figure divides by 33.7, written into the trip record. A trip anywhere else needs a base currency you choose, and the rate should either be fetched or clearly labelled as the rate you typed and when.

Part 3 — Four things only you can answer

These change what gets designed, so they're worth settling before the session rather than during it.

Scope

Is this for one real trip, or a product?

If you have a specific trip coming up, import (item 1) and reminders (item 9) matter far more than trips lists and recaps. If it's a product you'll keep, the order flips.

People

Who else will use it?

Just you, or family and Jelene too? Sharing (item 11) is a large piece of work and only worth it if someone else really is on the trip.

Budget

Will you pay for any APIs?

Place details, geocoding and live exchange rates all have free tiers with real limits and paid tiers that need a card. Your answer decides items 7, 19 and 24 — and whether Storage for full-size photos ever gets switched on.

Platforms

Does the native iOS app stay in step?

There are two implementations now. Keeping the SwiftUI one current roughly doubles every change and it still has never been compiled. My suggestion: let it sit, build on the web app, and revisit only if you get a Mac and want it in the App Store.

What I'd do in what order

  1. Items 17–24, now. No design needed and it removes the four things that read as broken. Give me the word and these are done before your design session ends.
  2. Item 1, import a trip. Nothing else matters if the app only holds a demo. Design this first and design it properly.
  3. Items 2 and 3, empty states and deletion. Together they're the difference between a prototype and something you'd trust with a real itinerary.
  4. Items 4, 5 and 6 — notes, multiple loops, and time. Three model decisions that get more expensive the longer they wait.
  5. Item 9, reminders. The highest ratio of usefulness to work in the whole list.
  6. Everything else, once you've used the app on a real day and know which of it you actually miss.
