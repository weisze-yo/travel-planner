TRAVEL PLANNER · P1 · DESIGN FOR REVIEW
Share → Join → Review, as one flow
Eleven surfaces, one round trip. The screens were already sound; five things were missing or wrong, and all five are in the seams. Canonical spec: docs/design/p1-share-join-review-flow-design.md — every string here is from its §11, and everything else is sample trip data.
F-1 · THE DOOR CLOSES
Later sets a module flag that is never reset, and go('review') exists in exactly one place — on the banner it hides. Tap it and the update is unreachable until the app restarts.
F-2 · THE JOIN IS SILENT
Making your copy… is assigned and then rendered nowhere. The button stays live through a multi-second write that creates a trip and a row per day, place, route and shot.
F-3 · ROLE IS FROZEN
A link's expiry can be changed after it is handed out. Its role cannot — there is no setLinkRole. The one that matters more is the one that is fixed.
F-4 · THE EXPLANATION IS BEHIND THE DECISION
The three-bullet What they get card renders only once sharing is on. The person deciding whether to share never sees the clearest statement of the copy model in the app.
RC-1 · THE DOCS WERE WRONG
The join preview does not follow the selected day. firstDay() picks the first day with stops — deterministic on every phone. D-4 closes with a label, not a behaviour change.

OD-4 · MING'S PROPOSAL — INVITE BY EMAIL INSTEAD OF A LINK
Owner types an email address · that person sees the trip when they sign in with it · no copy-paste · the share stays available until the owner removes them.
This is a product decision, not a layout one, so it is recorded rather than absorbed. It changes three things this batch treats as fixed: the link becomes an invite (no expiry chips, no opens count, no Copy); who can join becomes a named list instead of whoever holds the URL; and "until the owner removes them" reads as continuing access, which is the live-sync model the copy model deliberately replaced.
Resolved in review: design both, decide in development. Both are designed in §F below — one Share screen, one join, two doors onto it. Email is the better front door (nothing to paste, nothing to expire, revocable by name) and needs a backend address lookup and a notification path that do not exist yet; the link needs neither. Full write-up: overnight-p1-review-pack.md §5, OD-4.

Role is now a per-person chip and nothing else — which is where P0-1 always put it. The link's three facts collapse to one line with Change it reopening the sheet, so the role row (F-3) and the expiry live there rather than on the screen. "What they get" — display only, so can it hide?
Yes, it is display-only, and yes — after the first read. It stays open the first time, because it is the only place the app says that sharing hands over a copy rather than a live document, and that is the one thing the owner must know before tapping either share button. From then on it is a What they get › row that opens the three bullets as a sheet.
The summary sentence above it stays visible even when collapsed — one line, not three bullets, so the model is never entirely hidden. Collapsed state persists per trip.
Why role moved, and what it costs
Ming is right that a standing SENDING UPDATES section reads as a setting, when it is really a property of one act of sharing. Moving it into the sheet makes the Share screen shorter, removes the radios a non-owner should never see, and puts the choice next to the name it applies to.
Nothing in P0-1 changes but placement. Both options, the invariant line and the per-person chips keep their approved copy. The owner's own row is OWNER/YOU and has no chip — an owner can always send, and that was never adjustable.
The cost: the offer phase no longer explains the two roles before you commit to sharing at all. Accepted — the sheet is one tap away and is where the question is actually asked, and it is cancellable.
