Before the frames

The place card is the Destination screen

Decided: one thing at two heights, not a separate surface.

Batch 1 drew the place card from memory and got about a third of it. Reading src/screens/dest.js end to end settles the handoff's open question: everything a place card needs, the Destination screen already has — the badges, the name in three levels, both map buttons, the NO POSITION strip, five tabs with counts, the facts table, and its three editors. A second surface would be a second copy of all of it, and this repo's own rule is that a second copy is where bugs come from.

  1. Same markup, same tabs, same actions, at either height. The sheet shows the top of the page; pulling it up shows the rest of the same page. The peek is not a summary.
  2. From a Map pin it opens at half height (frame 19), because the map is what you are reading it against. From a Plan row it opens at full height (frame 20), because a row tap has always meant "open this place" — today's code already does exactly that.
  3. Two heights, not three. Half and full. The Map's own list keeps its three detents (frame 18); the place card replaces the list while it is open and gives it back when closed.
  4. The editors open only at full height, as the modals they are today. Nothing edits from a half-open sheet.
  5. Batch 1's frames 11 and 12 are withdrawn. Their ON YOUR PLAN row does not exist in the app — for a stop, the time badge already says it. The Must-place version of that question belongs to batch 5, where Must is designed.

  13. 13 · Question — the small grey labels are too faint. --faint (#9C9481) measures 2.87:1 on the cream in light mode and 3.64:1 in dark; the rule is 4.5:1. Decisions in batch two

Eight things, and why

The place card is Destination, at two heights

Half height from a pin, full height from a Plan row, one render. Everything it needed was already built and tested; a second surface would have been a second copy to drift.

Warnings and refusals are brown, never red or pink

The old strip was amber and the old refusal rust; neither survives the five colours. Brown is already the colour for "this matters" (emergency), and pink would say "press me". Only the fix is pink, because it is a button.

The archive is a sunken dashed card, not a dark one

Dark brown is kept for the show-this card and emergency. A removed stop is "set aside", so it sinks into the page instead — and it can no longer render white-on-white (M-4).

The only dark block on the Plan is the swipe track

It appears for the one irreversible act on the screen, and nowhere else. Its word is pale pink, like the emergency strip's, because the two mean the same thing: be sure.

A pressed toggle is pink

The pencil in edit mode is a pink tick, not green. It is a function in a selected state, the same rule as the active tab and a selected chip. Green stays the plan's own colour.

The day title moved into the trip header

D-2 puts "Day 4 of 6 · Saturday 11 April" on every tab, so the Plan's own Day 4 title would say it twice. The day pills take its place.

Tap the lit day pill to see the whole trip

The Map shows the whole trip (D-10) and a day pill flies in to one day. Getting back out needs no new button: the pill you are on is the way back.

A timed booking uses up free time

No sub route is offered across the train. The source does not know this yet; batch 4 has to write the rule into how lanes are built.

What I need from you before batch three

  1. The place card (frames 19–21). Is it right that it is simply the Destination page at two heights — half from the Map, full from the Plan? This is the one every later batch uses.
  2. Brown for warnings and refusals (frames 03 and 11). The five colours have no warning colour, so I chose brown. Say if you would rather have something else.
  3. The archive losing its dark card (frames 06 and 10). Sunken and dashed instead.
  4. The KTX question (finding 9). I have assumed a booked train leaves no free time. If that is right, batch 4 builds on it.

Say what to change and I will fix it inside batch three rather than holding the queue. Next up: batch three — Money: Shop, adding an expense, the split row, settling up, and Packing.
