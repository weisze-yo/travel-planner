2Shared plan, private progress
THE RULE THE WHOLE THING HANGS ON
The plan is ours. What I've done about it is mine.

Everything anyone changes about the schedule reaches everyone: stops, times, order, places, and a place's Must-see. Nothing about a person's progress does: what you've bought, what you've packed, and everything in your Log stay on your side and are never visible to anyone.

Between those sits the shopping list, which is neither. It is copied once, at the moment you first turn sharing on, and then both lists live their own lives. That has to be said out loud on the share sheet (2a), because a list that looks identical on two phones and quietly stops matching is worse than one that never matched.

This removes attribution from almost everywhere. No faces on the shopping list, no names in the Log, no avatar stack in a header. A name appears in exactly one place: the schedule changed, and here is who changed it.

WHAT I'D PUSH BACK ON

Two souvenir warnings are now gone: nobody can see that Ana bought the tenugui, and after the copy nobody sees her add it either. You've decided that gets sorted by talking, and the design says so plainly rather than implying a coordination it doesn't have. If it bites on the real trip, the smallest fix is one shared line per item — not syncing the list.

1Sharing one trip · invite, roles, attribution — superseded in part by turn 2
MY RECOMMENDATION
A link, one per trip, that expires

Your travellers are already in a group chat. A link goes where that conversation is and asks nobody to type anything. A code needs a screen to type it into and a keyboard on a phone, which is a whole flow to build and explain; email needs addresses you may not have and lands in Promotions. The link carries the trip and the role — not your account — so it can be turned off, and it expires on its own if you forget.

The other reason is identity. With a link you collect it at the moment of accepting, on their phone, in whichever way they already sign in. You never have to know how your uncle does email.

Before accepting, they see the trip and not a signup: name, dates, who invited them, what they will be allowed to do, and a real read-only look at Day 1 (1c). Most of them have never seen this app, so the first screen has to prove it is a schedule.

TWO LEVELS, AND ONE OWNER

Agreed: can edit and can read. The only third level worth anything would be "can add notes but not move stops", and that is a paragraph of explanation for a case that resolves itself socially. What makes two levels enough is that owner is not a level — it is you. Only the owner invites, changes a role, removes a person, or deletes the trip, so an editor can be generous without being dangerous.

WHERE A NAME APPEARS
Only where two people can collide, or where the words are the point. That is shopping and notes. Everything else stays clean.
Shopping says it in words, not faces. An unclaimed row carries nothing. A claimed row says Ana's getting this. A bought row moves out of the buying list. No avatar grid — the row you can still act on is the row with no name on it.
Notes carry an author because a note is somebody talking. Initial, name, time, once per note.
Packing is nobody's business. Each person's bag is their own list, attributed once at the top, never per item.
Times and stops carry no name until two edits disagree — then, and only then, the row says who moved it (1j).
People are one grey chip with an initial, so eight travellers and 中文 names both fit.
