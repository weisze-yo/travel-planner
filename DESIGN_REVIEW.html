Settle these five first

Not because they are the biggest screens, but because everything else is drawn differently depending on how they land.

25
Model

"Stop" and "place" are still two different things

Carried over, and this round made it more visible rather than less. Pasting a map link now creates a place hanging off a stop; typing a stop into the Plan creates an itinerary row. They never join up, so a place you saved cannot become a stop without being retyped, and a stop you typed can never hold the opening hours a place carries.

Shopping items still key off a place name as text, so renaming a stop orphans its items — and the destination Shop tab, which matches on that name, then shows nothing.

To design One model, as a diagram before any screen. My recommendation is unchanged: everything is a place, and the itinerary is an ordered list of visits to places, each with a time and a route colour. Then a sub route is just visits you added, and shopping, shots and notes all hang off the place — which is what turn 1 promised with "the place is the atom".
04
Model

A day still holds only one note

The destination screen now has a Log tab, which makes the problem plain rather than hidden: it can only show the day's single note, and only when that note happens to be filed under this stop. The tab says so in as many words, which is honest and unsatisfying.

To design Notes belonging to a stop, with the Log day card stacking that day's notes under the stop name. Falls out of item 25 if that is settled first.
05
Model

Free time can still only happen once a day

Unchanged. One sub route per day, hanging off one stop. Places are correctly per-stop now, so every stop can have candidates around it — but only one of them can have a loop built from them.

To design How the Plan shows two or three loops in one day, and how the Sub route screen names which stop it belongs to.
06
Serious

Times are text, and a stop's window still can't be edited

The buffer now reads the coach time off the anchor stop's window, which makes that window load-bearing — and there is still no way to edit it. Edit mode exposes only a stop's start time. Durations remain fixed strings, and dragging can still put 09:15 after 18:00 without comment.

To design How a stop's start and end are entered — one time plus a duration, or two times — and what an out-of-order or overlapping day looks like. A warning on the row rather than a refused drag.
01
Serious

Getting a real itinerary inprogressed

There is now a trips home, a new-trip form, editable dates and length, and stops you can type. So the app is usable for your trip. What is missing is still the part that makes it bearable: typing six days of stops on a phone when the itinerary already exists as a PDF or a WhatsApp message.

Worth designing to the constraint: with no paid API and no server, parsing happens on the device or by hand. So the realistic screen is paste-and-confirm, not magic.

To design A paste-the-itinerary screen: text in, candidate stops with times out, each row confirmed or corrected before it lands. Design the half-understood case, because that is the normal one.

29
New

Three things I built plain, which now need a designernew

To unblock you I built the trips home, the spend report and the swipe-to-delete pattern in the app's existing language rather than waiting for artboards. They work and they are consistent, but nobody designed them.

Specifically: the trip card shows a two-letter mark, name, dates and length — no cover image, no sense of past versus upcoming, nothing about how ready the trip is. The report is a hero figure, one bar per bucket and a list. The swipe reveals a single red dustbin at a fixed 68px.

To design These three, on top of what exists — I would rather rebuild them to a design than have them stay as my improvisation. The trip card is the one that matters most, since it is now the first thing you see.
09
Serious

The app knows you'll miss the coach and never tells you

Unchanged, and still the best ratio of usefulness to work in the list. The buffer sits on a screen you have to remember to open. Same for the 14:00 demo, stalls shutting at 17:00, and the last-chance airport items.

Buildable within your constraints: an installed home-screen web app can send notifications on iOS 16.4 and later, with no server.

To design Where a reminder is set — per stop, per shopping item, or one trip setting — and what it says when it fires.
08
Serious

Must-see spots can be ticked, but not added

Sharper again this round. Shots now correctly belong to one stop, so your real trip has none and no way to create any — the four demo shots belong to the demo market. Every stop's Must-see tab reads "nothing noted yet · adding your own is still to come", which is an honest label for a hole.

To design Adding a spot — name, where to stand, best time — attaching a reference image, and the card once your own photo sits beside the reference. Without Cloud Storage photos are 320px thumbnails, so do not design for full-bleed images.
10
Serious

A shopping item still can't be correctedprogressed

You can now delete an item, tag it with a category, and filter the list by day and place. You still cannot rename one, change its estimate, move it to a different shop, or give it a quantity — so a typo means delete and retype.

To design The edit state of an item. Quantity is worth deciding on at the same time, since it changes how a price reads.
27
Serious

Offline means the map still goes blank

Unchanged. Trip data works offline now; map tiles are images fetched per view. Tiles you have already looked at are cached, so areas you browsed at the hotel still draw — pan somewhere new with no signal and you get grey. Which is the situation the sub route exists for.

To design A "download this area" action: where it lives, how the area is chosen (the day's stops plus a margin), what it says about size, and what an undownloaded area shows.
28
Serious

You still can't tell whether your changes are saved

Three states, one of them shown. Online and syncing: silent. Offline with the cache: edits queue and replay, also silent, and indistinguishable from the first. Configured but unable to reach Firebase: changes are stranded in that browser, and I show a red banner because silence there is dangerous.

That banner is still my improvisation. For an app used in a market with bad signal, knowing whether the ticked-off list actually saved is not a detail.

To design Syncing, queued-offline and stranded: ignorable when all is well, unmissable when it is not.
07
Serious

What place information is worth typingprogressed

Partly answered by the work: pasting a map link now pulls opening hours, a phone number and a website from OpenStreetMap when it has them, which costs nothing. Coverage is volunteer data, so most places will come back with a position and little else.

So the eight-row table stands mostly empty for anywhere you added yourself, and there is no way to fill a row by hand.

To design Which rows earn their keep, how a mostly-empty table reads, and whether a single free-text "what I need to know" beats eight labelled rows for your own places.
11
Serious

You cannot travel with anyone

Unchanged and still unanswered — you told me the trip is yours but not who else is on it. Sign-in is anonymous, so a trip belongs to one browser: your phone and your laptop hold unrelated trips, and clearing Safari's data starts another.

To design Only if someone else is coming: sign-in plus sharing — who can edit, what an invite looks like, whether a shared list shows who ticked what. If it is just you, sign-in alone still matters, so a cleared browser cannot lose the trip.

Most of these now exist in plain form — the empty day, the stop with nothing saved around it, the stop with no shots, the trip with no trips. They are functional, not designed.

The first-launch question is now sharper: the app still seeds a demo trip so the screens have content, and you have to open trip settings to empty it. With a trips home in place, a first run could simply offer "start a trip" beside "look around the demo".

Two things still unanswered

People

Who else is on this trip?

It decides item 11 entirely — whether sharing is a large piece of design work, or just sign-in so a cleared browser cannot lose your itinerary.

Platforms

Is the native iOS app parked?

It has never been compiled and now trails the web app by three rounds of work — the trips home, the destination panels, the spend report, swipe-to-delete and the whole offline layer exist only on the web. My suggestion stands: leave it, build on the web app, revisit if you get a Mac. Say so and I will stop mentioning it.

What I'd design in what order

  1. Item 25, the model — one diagram of places, visits and what hangs off which. Items 4, 5, 7 and 8 all resolve more cleanly afterwards, and screens drawn before it get drawn twice.
  2. Items 4, 5 and 6 — notes per stop, more than one loop a day, and how a stop's start and end are entered. Three decisions that fall out of the model.
  3. Item 1, pasting an itinerary in — the difference between the app being pleasant on this trip and merely possible.
  4. Item 29, the three screens I improvised — the trip card first, since it is now the first thing you see when the app opens.
  5. Items 9, 27 and 28 — reminders, offline map areas, and showing whether changes saved. The three that make it trustworthy on the road.
  6. The rest once you have used it for a day and know what you actually reached for.
