Design review · round six · all three design sessions, built

What the Travel Planner still needs

All three design sessions are built and deployed. The day owns its times and its loops and can be filled by pasting an itinerary in; the trips home, the spend report and the swipe are drawn rather than improvised; the app now says when to leave, keeps a patch of map on the phone and admits when a change has not reached the cloud; and a trip can be shared on the rule that the plan is ours and what I've done about it is mine. Thirteen things are still open — the fifteen that were, less the five now built, plus three the sharing work created. Those three are the honest edges of what sharing can promise two people without a server, and the first of them is a decision rather than a drawing.

  • 29 The trips home is a status board. A running trip gets a cover, the day it is on and the next thing on it; an upcoming one says how ready it is and how many days away; a finished one carries its recap. The cover can be a photo from the Log, one of four tints, or a picture from the phone.
  • 29 The spend report is drawn, not improvised. A dark hero, a per-day chart that doubles as the filter, where it went, guessed against paid, cash against card, and every purchase grouped by day with the gaps named.
  • 29 The swipe has five positions, not one: closed, following, latched at 88px with the word, decisive past 60% of the row, and the in-row confirmation. Every deletion leaves an undo bar for six seconds, from one slot shared by the whole app.
  • 09 The app says when to leave. One strip above the tab bar, empty nearly all day: amber under fifteen minutes of slack, red when the walk back equals the time left, and silent before departure and once the deadline has passed. Said once per deadline, and back only if the walk gets ten minutes longer.
  • 27 A patch of map is kept on the phone. Draw a box, choose streets or doorways, see the size before you commit. Two requests at a time, over the box you drew, and refused outright above 1,500 tiles — OpenStreetMap's servers are a volunteer's, so bulk is refused rather than throttled. The tile cache is deliberately un-versioned, so a deploy never wipes a download.
  • 28 You can tell whether your changes are saved. A ledger in localStorage holds what is outstanding, so "stuck for three days" is true across launches. Five states, one of them loud: saving, saved, queued, stuck, and local-only. The stuck screen says why, what would be lost, how many times it tried, and offers a copy that is not on this phone.
  • 11 A trip can be shared, on one rule: the plan is ours, what I've done about it is mine. The schedule syncs and last edit wins; the shopping list is copied once and then separate, with the owner able only ever to add; bought, packed, the Log and the photos never leave the phone, and packing is not in the share at all. A link per trip with an expiry, two levels plus owner, sign-in at the last possible moment, and a day that names what moved and who moved it rather than asking you to resolve it.

The model, as built Items 25, 04, 05 and 06, all settled — draw against this rather than around it.

There is one kind of record. A place is a name, a position, a category and whatever OpenStreetMap knows about it. A visit is that place on a day, at a time, on a route — which is what the itinerary shows and what everyone calls a stop. A place that hangs off another place is what the Nearby list shows. Same record, same screen, same creation form.

place name · position · category · hours, phone, website · source link │ ├── anchored to another place, or nothing if it stands alone ├── nearby every place anchored to this one ├── must-see shots to get here ├── shopping what to buy here └── notes one per place per day — item 04, now built day ├── visits ordered: place + start + length → end is derived └── loops as many as the day has gaps — item 05, now built start place · end place · places in between depart · return by → time to spend

Two consequences worth designing to. A place saved from a Nearby list is already a candidate stop — it needs no retyping, only a day and a time. And shopping, shots, notes and the loops all point at the place by identity, so renaming it no longer orphans anything.

Two more, added this round. A visit stores its start and its length and never its end, so a window cannot disagree with itself — which is why every screen that shows 13:30 – 15:45 is computing it. And a loop is a sibling of the visits rather than a child of one: it hangs off the day, names the stop it happens inside, and there can be several.

Settle these first

The three sessions took everything they were drawn for. What is left is three new items the sharing work created — each one an edge of what "no paid APIs and it must work offline" can actually promise two people — plus the six that were always going to wait. These three are the ones to answer before anyone uses the share on a real trip.

31
New

Sharing needs a server the free plan does not give itnew

The whole of session three is built and works against one trip document: the people, the roles, the link, the change feed, the copied-once shopping list, and the promise that the Log never leaves the phone. Two phones reading and writing the same document behave exactly as designed — I verified that by running both sides against one store.

What is missing sits underneath. Firestore's free tier will hold the document, but opening exactly one trip to exactly the people invited to it needs security rules keyed on a real signed-in identity, and Apple and Google sign-in both need a client id and a redirect this app cannot own from a static host. So today the sign-in screen collects the name it needs and says so plainly, rather than pretending to be OAuth.

To decide, not design Whether to stand up the Firebase project properly (free, but it needs your Google account and a domain for the redirect), or to keep sharing as one-device-per-trip and exchange a trip as a file — which the stuck screen's "Save a copy" already does. Everything drawn in session three works either way; only who can reach the document changes.
32
New

Two phones with no signal both winnew

Last edit wins is the right rule and it is what is built. The case it does not cover is the one this trip is most likely to hit: both phones offline in a market, both editing Day 3, both writing when signal returns. The later write lands second and wins — not the later edit. So the person who reconnects last wins, which is not the same thing and is not what the banner promises.

The banner does still tell the truth about what moved, and the fix is the one the design already names: change it back and they see the same banner tomorrow. But a six-hour-old edit overwriting a fresh one is worth knowing about before it happens.

To design Whether a write that is older than the value it replaces should say so — a mark on the row, or a line in the banner — and whether that is worth a timestamp per field rather than per document.

New

Nobody can see that Ana bought the tenuguinew

Your call, and the design says it out loud rather than implying a coordination it does not have: the shopping list is copied once, and after that nobody sees anyone else's additions or purchases. The guest's list carries one line saying where it came from, and the owner's sheet says how many items the other side does not have.

Now that it is built, the shape of the gap is clearer than it was on paper. It is not symmetrical: the owner can send new items and knows they arrived, while a guest who adds something has no way to tell anyone. So the person who made the trip keeps a channel and everyone else does not.

To design, if it bites The smallest fix is still one shared line per item rather than syncing the list — and the second smallest is letting a guest send items back the same way the owner sends them out. Worth leaving alone until a real trip proves it needs one.

Serious

A pasted itinerary has no positions, so the map is emptysharper

Pasting six days in takes about a minute and gives you every stop, every time and every length — and not one coordinate, because reading a map link is per-place and needs the network. So on a real trip the Plan is complete and the Map is blank, which is the opposite way round from the demo.

Sharper now that keeping a map area is built, because that feature has to draw its box around something. Today it falls back to the trip's own position and says how many stops are inside; on a freshly pasted trip that number is zero, and the box is a guess. The same hole shows up in the leave-now strip, which needs a walking distance it cannot compute without positions.

To design How an unlocated stop reads on the Plan and on the Map, where the count lives, and whether there is a way to work through them in one pass. Worth deciding whether locating a stop is even worth doing for all of them, or only for the ones you will walk from.

What I'd design in what order

The three sessions are done. What is left has no session shape to it, so this is simply the order I would take it in.

  1. Item 31 first, and it is a decision rather than a drawing. Whether sharing gets a real backend or stays a file you hand someone. Everything drawn in session three works either way, but items 32 and 33 only matter if the answer is a backend.
  2. Item 30, the pasted itinerary with no positions. It is now the thing most other features trip over: the map area has nothing to draw a box around, and the leave-now strip has no walk to measure.
  3. Items 8 and 10 — adding a must-see spot, and correcting a shopping item you got wrong in the shop. Both are small holes in screens that otherwise work.
  4. Items 7 and 12 — what place information is worth typing, and the second language. Twelve gets wider with every screen and this round added six of them.
  5. The rest — dark mode, empty states, the accessibility pass, search, the recap — once you have used it for a day and know what you actually reached for.
How this was checked. Round six was driven in a browser at 390×844 like the five before it: 145 checks across three harnesses — 56 for the trips home, the spend report and the swipe, 56 for the strip, the offline map and the sync ledger, 89 for sharing — all passing, with no console errors on any screen. The reminder rules were run against a real schedule at four different clock times and silenced and re-raised as designed. The tile maths was checked against the trip's own box: 300 tiles for streets, 4,018 for doorways, and the second refused as bulk. A real download of twelve tiles was run end to end and the tiles were found afterwards in the cache the service worker serves from. A ledger three days old was planted and the strip, the stuck screen and the discard were driven through to an empty queue. Sharing was checked with both sides running against one trip document, which is the shape a backend would give them: a link was made as can-read expiring in 24 hours, opened on a second identity, joined, signed in — and the anonymous identity carried through rather than being replaced. The owner then moved a stop, and the reader's Day 3 named the change, who made it and what it used to say, cleared it on Got it, and offered the whole record. The share snapshot was inspected directly and carries no Log, no packing list and nothing marked bought. A removal was run and the kept-side trip came out with the lists and the Log and none of the stops. What that turned up: five bugs, all listed above and all fixed — nothing listening to the sync ledger, a warning about an area state that could not exist, a date formatter that threw inside a render, a removed trip listed twice, and an unnamed owner reading as "You keeps this trip". What that did not cover: the three free services — weather, geocoding, exchange rates — are blocked from this sandbox, so their parsers were verified against recorded payloads rather than live calls. Tiles were served locally rather than from OpenStreetMap, so the cap and the two-lane pacing are verified but the real servers' behaviour is not. And sharing has no backend yet, which is item 31: two phones were simulated against one document rather than actually being two phones. Still not covered: anything that only shows up on a real trip with real signal — and, for the parser, any itinerary shaped differently from the ones I could invent. It will misread lines; the confirm screen is the answer to that, not the parser.
