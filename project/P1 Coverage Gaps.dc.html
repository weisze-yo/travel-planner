Remaining coverage gaps
Five items chosen from the coverage matrix on one rule: user-facing, undesigned, cheap to specify, and likely to produce inconsistent UX if left. Two of them turned out to need recording, not designing. Canonical spec: docs/design/p1-coverage-gaps-design.md — §7 is the only new copy.
1 · Tile download — a good flow with no ending
What this is, and where it happens. A tile is one small square of the map image. Keeping an area offline means downloading every tile that covers it — a few hundred of them — so the map still draws with no signal abroad. It happens on Map ▸ Kept maps ▸ an area ▸ Keep this area on my phone: the user draws or accepts a box, picks a detail level, and taps Keep it. The bar below is that download running, in the area's own screen; the jade receipt lands on Kept maps, which is where the download returns to. Nothing else in the app downloads tiles.

OD-5 · MING: "WHAT DOES SHORT BY MEAN?"
A sub route is a walk you fit inside a gap in the day — leave a stop, visit a few places, be back by a time. The three tiles are that gap's arithmetic: YOU HAVE the whole gap, TRAVELLING is the walking inside it, and the third tile is what is left. When it is positive it is TO SPEND. When it is negative the walk does not fit, and the label today is SHORT BY — meaning you are 25 minutes short of the time this walk needs.
The question is the finding. The lead read the label and could not tell whether 25 minutes was missing or spare — and this is existing shipped copy, so it is recorded rather than changed. Recommendation: OVER BY 25 min. The number is the overshoot either way, and "over" is unambiguous against "to spend" in a way that "short" is not. The sentence below it already explains itself and does not change.

4 · Two deliberate nothings
Cross-tab sync gets no announcement
P0-5 R5 — work the user did not start gets no indicator. A second tab is not an event; it is the same person, and the correct outcome is that the two tabs simply agree. One requirement, not a new element: if the other tab clears the outbox while this one shows the rust stuck state, the whole screen re-derives from syncState(), header included — which is what the matrix's "cross-tab sync, never designed" row was actually asking about.
The warning strip's rank order, written down at last
