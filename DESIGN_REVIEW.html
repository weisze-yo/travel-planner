What the Travel Planner still needs

The four that had to be settled first are built. The day now owns its times, holds as many stretches of free time as it has gaps, keeps a note per place rather than one per day, and can be filled by pasting the agent's itinerary in. Fifteen things are still open — the eighteen that were, less the four now built, plus one this round's work created — and they divide cleanly between the two design sessions left.

Each is its own record with its own name, ends and times; each gets its own dashed row on the Plan, placed by when it departs and swipeable left to the red bin. The model, as built Items 25, 04, 05 and 06, all settled — draw against this rather than around it.

There is one kind of record. A place is a name, a position, a category and whatever OpenStreetMap knows about it. A visit is that place on a day, at a time, on a route — which is what the itinerary shows and what everyone calls a stop. A place that hangs off another place is what the Nearby list shows. Same record, same screen, same creation form.

place name · position · category · hours, phone, website · source link │ ├── anchored to another place, or nothing if it stands alone ├── nearby every place anchored to this one ├── must-see shots to get here ├── shopping what to buy here └── notes one per place per day — item 04, now built day ├── visits ordered: place + start + length → end is derived └── loops as many as the day has gaps — item 05, now built start place · end place · places in between depart · return by → time to spend

Two consequences worth designing to. A place saved from a Nearby list is already a candidate stop — it needs no retyping, only a day and a time. And shopping, shots, notes and the loops all point at the place by identity, so renaming it no longer orphans anything.

Two more, added this round. A visit stores its start and its length and never its end, so a window cannot disagree with itself — which is why every screen that shows 13:30 – 15:45 is computing it. And a loop is a sibling of the visits rather than a child of one: it hangs off the day, names the stop it happens inside, and there can be several.

New

A pasted itinerary has no positions, so the map is emptynew

The consequence of item 01 landing. Pasting six days in takes about a minute and gives you every stop, every time and every length — and not one coordinate, because reading a map link is per-place and needs the network. So on a real trip the Plan is complete and the Map is blank, which is the opposite way round from the demo.

The screen says so, and the route to fixing one stop exists: open it, paste its map link. What is missing is any sense of how many are still unlocated, or a way through them that is not eighteen separate visits to eighteen screens.

To design How an unlocated stop reads on the Plan and on the Map, where the count lives, and whether there is a way to work through them in one pass. Worth deciding whether locating a stop is even worth doing for all of them, or only for the ones you will walk from — which is the question item 27 asks from the other side.
28
Session 2

You still can't tell whether your changes are savedunchanged

Three states, one of them shown. Online and syncing: silent. Offline with the cache: edits queue and replay, also silent, and indistinguishable from the first. Configured but unable to reach Firebase: changes are stranded in that browser, and I show a red banner because silence there is dangerous.

This round makes it matter more, not less. Pasting an itinerary in is the largest single write the app ever makes — eighteen stops and a dozen places at once — and it is exactly the sort of thing you would do on hotel wifi the night before.

To design Syncing, queued-offline and stranded: ignorable when all is well, unmissable when it is not. And what the paste screen says if the write cannot leave the device.

Session 3

Sharing one trip with the people on itunchanged

The largest remaining piece, and the whole of session three. Your answer stands: family and friends, aligned on the schedule, one selected trip rather than everything you have.

The plumbing is mine and I will build it: real sign-in, linking the anonymous account you already have so nothing is lost, and rules that open exactly one trip to exactly the people invited to it. What I cannot invent is how it looks or how much anyone else may touch.

To design Four things. The invite — a link, a code, or an email, and what the person sees before they accept. The share sheet on a trip, listing who has it and letting you take it back. What a guest can do — my recommendation is still two levels only, can-edit and can-read, because three is a settings screen nobody reads. And attribution: whether a ticked shopping item, a packed bag or a note shows who did it. Design the shopping list with two people on it, because that is where it gets confusing first — and now the Log too, since a day holds several notes and two people will write them.

Trip data works offline now; map tiles are images fetched per view. Tiles you have already looked at are cached, so areas you browsed at the hotel still draw — pan somewhere new with no signal and you get grey. Which is the situation the loops exist for.

Sharper because of item 30. "The day's stops plus a margin" was the obvious way to choose an area, and a pasted itinerary has no stops with positions — so on a real trip there is nothing to draw the box around until the stops are located. The two items have to be answered together.

What I'd design in what order

Session one took items 4, 5, 6 and 1 — the day's model. What is left maps onto the two sessions you have named, in this order.

  1. Session two, first half — item 29, the three screens I improvised. The trip card first, since it is the first thing you see when the app opens, and it can now say something true about whether a trip is ready.
  2. Session two, second half — items 9, 27, 28 and 30. Reminders, offline map areas, showing whether changes saved, and what an unlocated stop looks like. The four that decide whether the app is trustworthy on the road, and they interlock: a pasted itinerary has no positions, which is what 30 is about and what 27 has to work around.
  3. Session three — item 11, sharing one trip. The largest single piece: invite, share sheet, two permission levels, and what attribution shows. Design the shopping list and the Log with two people on them, because that is where it gets confusing first.
  4. Items 7, 8, 10 and 12 alongside whichever session touches their screens — the place table, adding a shot, correcting a shopping item, and the second language.
  5. The rest — dark mode, empty states, the accessibility pass, search, the recap — once you have used it for a day and know what you actually reached for.
How this was checked. Round five was driven in a browser at 390×844 like the four before it: 56 checks, all passing, plus a pass over every screen and every destination panel with no console errors. The three migrations were run against the existing trip and their results counted — 13:30 – 15:45 became 135 minutes and the old strings were cleared, the day's loop adopted the row that had stood for it implicitly, both notes gained a place and a written-at time — then run again to confirm they add nothing the second time. A stop was retimed and its length changed, and the end moved with it; a stop was dragged to sit after one four hours later, and the row said so rather than refusing; a stop was stretched until it swallowed two others, and both said so. A second loop was started on day 3, landed in a real gap rather than on top of the first, was renamed, took a place that the first did not, and was deleted with its row and without its places. Two notes were written about two places on one day and both survived; a third on another day showed up under the same place's Log tab. Eleven lines of mixed English and Chinese itinerary were pasted, read as seven stops across two days with one flagged, corrected in place, and landed on days 5 and 6 with their ranges turned into lengths. What that turned up: two bugs, both listed above and both fixed — a migrated note pointing at a replaced id, and day-scoped Nearby asking by the wrong id. What that did not cover: the three free services — weather, geocoding, exchange rates — are blocked from this sandbox, so their parsers were verified against recorded payloads rather than live calls. They are still the first thing to try on your phone. Still not covered: anything that only shows up on a real trip with real signal — and, for the parser, any itinerary shaped differently from the ones I could invent. It will misread lines; the confirm screen is the answer to that, not the parser.
