Three screens that were built plain
WHAT EACH ONE IS FOR
A trip has a state, spend has a shape, and a swipe has a threshold

Trips home is a status board, not a list. A trip is either running, coming up or finished, and each of those wants a different card: the running one shows what is next, the upcoming one shows how ready you are, the finished one shows what it came to. The cover comes from a photo already in the trip's log or from your phone — the app cannot invent one, so the fallback is a plain tinted field with the trip's mark.

The spend report gains two things a total and a category bar cannot say: how the money moved across the days, and how close your estimates were. Those are the two questions you actually ask when you get home.

The swipe row keeps the decision you already made — swipe, dustbin, confirm — and pins down the three numbers it was missing: where the dustbin latches, where a full swipe takes over, and what confirm looks like without a dialog on top of the list.

CHANGED FROM WHAT IS BUILT
Trips grouped by state
On now · Coming up · Finished, in that order, with the running trip given a cover-width card.
Readiness, not a progress bar
Three counts you can act on: empty days, packing, money. No single score.
Spend gets a day chart
Category and payment stay, but below the shape of the trip.
Red track follows the finger
The panel is no longer a fixed 68px window with a floating dustbin.

THE SAME EVERYWHERE
Flat rows keep square corners and the red runs edge to edge; cards keep their 14–15px radius and the red is clipped to it. Rows taller than 88px centre the dustbin. A row that cannot be deleted does not move at all — no rubber band, no red.
