1Waypoint — first pass: information architecture + core screens
MY ASSUMPTIONS — CORRECT ME
One trip, two kinds of route

Your brief has one structural idea underneath it: the agent owns the spine of the day, and you own the gaps. So the whole app is built on that split. The main route is fixed, numbered and solid green — you can read it but not move it. Every stop on it exposes its slack ("2h 15m free"), and that slack is the doorway to everything you plan yourself: nearby places, shopping stops, photo spots. Your own additions are always dashed and amber, so at a glance you know what is committed versus what is yours.

Design system attached to this project is empty, so I set the direction: Public Sans, a deep-jade / bone palette, jade for the agent, amber for you. Say the word and I'll swap it.

NAVIGATION

Five tabs: Map (home, per your pick — timeline rides on it as a sheet), Plan, 购物, 准备, Log. Destination detail, the free-time explorer and shot cards are pushed screens, not tabs — they always belong to a stop. Trips live behind the header chip, so adding a second trip later needs no new navigation.

HOW THE PIECES FEED EACH OTHER
The place is the atom

Rather than five unrelated lists, everything hangs off one object — a place. A place can carry practical info, shopping items, shot cards and gear requirements. That single relationship is what makes the app feel joined up:

1
Add a nearby place to a free-time loop → its shopping items appear in 购物清单 tagged with that day and time window.
2
A shot card's 穿搭 note pushes the garment into 出行准备, so packing is derived from the itinerary rather than remembered.
3
A place's opening hours are checked against the stop's time window — a shop that shuts at 16:00 can't be suggested for a 17:00 gap.
4
Every loop is time-checked against the coach departure and shows the buffer, so free time never becomes a missed bus.
5
Marking an item bought writes the real price into the budget; marking a shot taken feeds the Log. Planning becomes the log by being used.
