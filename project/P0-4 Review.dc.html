TRAVEL PLANNER · P0-4 · DESIGN FOR REVIEW
Review, redesigned
A retained base makes the screen truthful, the row stops wrapping at 390px, and the one row where both of you moved is the only one that carries weight. Canonical spec: docs/design/p0-4-review-design.md; every piece of copy here is from its §10, and everything else on these frames is sample trip data. New CSS in the whole design: .side.stacked and .review-group.
WHAT TWO-WAY GETS WRONG
Three rows reachable today name the other person as the author of your own work, and one of the two buttons destroys it: your edit reverted, your deleted stop re-added, your new stop deleted.
THE BASE ALREADY EXISTS
The sending side keeps share.snapshot and diffs against it. Three-way makes the two sides symmetric — one blob per trip — rather than adding a mechanism.
STAKES, NOT SYMMETRY
Only the row where both of you changed the same thing costs anything. It is the only row that gets a rust badge and a line naming what leaves.
UNDO, NOT CONFIRM
Decisions keep writing immediately and join the app's existing 6-second undo bar. The house rule finally applies to the screen it was missing from.

Four things this frame changes
STACKED SIDES
Full width, so a value has 318px instead of 150px. Two stacked one-line boxes replace two side-by-side boxes that each wrapped to two lines — the vertical cost is roughly neutral, and the wrapping is gone.
DAY GROUPING
One eyebrow per day replaces the Day 3 chip that currently repeats on every card. The card's context line carries position instead.
THE STICKY FOOT
.review-foot is already in app.css and unused. Today the bulk actions sit two screens below the fold, so the cheap way out of a long update is the hardest thing to find.
THE PREAMBLE MOVES
Same three sentences, under the foot's buttons instead of above the first row.
Where the base is written — three places, nowhere else
joinTrip()
the snapshot you were seeded from — at t0, base and mine are identical
finishReview()
the snapshot just reviewed — their state at that version, whatever you took from it
publishUpdate()
already writes share.snapshot; an owner's base is their last published snapshot
A side effect worth having: "whatever you skip is not asked about again" becomes structural. Keep yours at version V and at V+1 your side differs from base while theirs matches it — so it is not asked. It returns only if they change the same thing again, which is a genuinely new question.

THE UNDO BAR — ALREADY IN THE APP
Took theirs for Lumen Crossing
Undo
rememberUndo(label, restore) — 6 seconds, one app-wide bar, already used by place delete, stop delete, area remove and person remove. Review joins it; no new component. A row returns to the list when its decision is undone, or the undo is only half true.
Bulk gets one undo for the whole batch. finishReview is deliberately not undoable — it is the act of leaving, and every row was individually undoable at the time.

The safety rule, said in the app's own voice, at the moment it applies.
A bulk action may never decide a row where both of you changed the same thing
Keep all of mine — applies to every remaining row, conflicts included. It changes nothing on your copy, so it must never be gated: it is the "not now" exit.
Take the n you have not touched — only rows where mine equals base. On those, taking theirs discards nothing of yours. The count is in the label, so the button says how much it is doing. Absent when n = 0.
Take all of theirs — removed. Under three-way it would silently discard your own edits on exactly the rows the screen exists to protect, and nothing else in the app has a one-tap control that destroys work with no per-item view.
One-decision-per-difference was never "every row must be tapped" — it was "no single tap may resolve a difference whose stakes you have not seen". Both surviving bulk actions honour that.

What a decision records
Today both keepMine and takeChange end in the same markReviewed(), so which differences you dealt with persists — but not which way. That is the whole gap, and it needs one field, not a new mechanism:
trip.reviewed: [{ id, choice, title, outcome, at }]
…which pendingUpdate() filters on exactly as it filters declined today, and which finishReview moves into trip.lastReview instead of discarding.
Leaving mid-review needs no warning: decisions are already written and already persisted, and there is no dirty state to lose. The Plan banner's ghost Later exists for exactly that — what it does is D-1, still out of scope.
