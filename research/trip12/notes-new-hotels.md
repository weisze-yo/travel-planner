## 1. MISSION 2 — the yukata group photo. **IT STILL WORKS, and it gets easier.**

The company app pins Mission 2 to *"Ooedo Onsen Monogatari Premium Kinugawa Kanko Hotel /
Similar Class — Hotel Lobby, dinner time, dress code hotel-provided yukata."* That hotel is
gone. Hana no Yado Matsuya is the "similar class" successor and it satisfies the mission on
every clause bar one, which has to be settled at check-in.

**Does it lend yukata? Yes — and they are IN THE ROOM.**
Yukoyuko's room-amenity list for Matsuya carries `大人浴衣` and `子供浴衣`. That is the single
most useful finding of this whole revision, because the old hotel's yukata came off a rack
beside the front desk (`フロントの浴衣コーナーにて貸し出しをしております`) and 35 people
choosing and sizing colour yukata at a counter was identified as *the* bottleneck of Day 4.
**That bottleneck no longer exists.** Guests go to their rooms and change there.

There is also a paid upgrade: Jalan lists `浴衣レンタル（客室フロア45セット、3,300円）` — a
45-set yukata rental **on the guest-room floor**, ¥3,300. Forty-five sets against ~35 people
means ViTrox *can* have matching or deliberately-varied colour yukata for the shot if it wants
them, and the collection point is the guest-room floor, not the lobby. Worth pricing with the
agent: 35 × ¥3,300 ≈ ¥115,500.

*Caveat, stated plainly:* the in-room yukata is an amenity-table entry, not an operator
statement, and one channel (Yahoo Travel) prints `浴衣` as **not** supplied. `matsuya.co.jp`
refuses automated fetching from this sandbox (the egress proxy denies the connection outright,
same behaviour as `granbellhotel.jp` in the earlier session), so the operator's own page could
not be read to settle it. **Confirm on the phone.** Every Matsuya record is marked `medium`
for this reason.

**Where would the photo stand? The 5th floor.**
Matsuya is built *down* the slope from Route 121, so you enter at the top: **the front desk and
the lounge are both on the 5th floor.** Two independent stay reports agree on what is up there —
a lounge named **花かんざし (Hana-kanzashi)** beside the front desk, a glass wall onto the
Kinugawa gorge, a library, Takehisa Yumeji paintings (Yumeji is in the lifts too) and a cabinet
of `夢二人形` dolls, plus a drink corner running 14:00–17:00 and a frozen-food corner. The 5F
shop 花ごよみ is the same lap of the room. As a *backdrop* this is far better than the old
hotel's front-desk-and-grand-piano wall: Taisho-era illustration, warm ryokan lighting, and a
theme the reel can actually be about.

**Standing position** (in `mustSee`, `n4mt-ms-mission2`): along the **gorge-side glass wall,
group facing INTO the room** so the Yumeji paintings and the dolls cabinet are behind them and
the black window is behind the *camera*. At 18:30 in September that glass is dead black and
would silhouette all 35 faces — the same trap as the old hotel's entrance doors. Three rows,
alternate the yukata colours along each row, obi knots to the front, left lapel over right,
hotel slippers, white balance locked around 3000–3200 K.

**Does the lobby take 35 people? UNVERIFIED, and this is the one open risk.**
No source publishes a floor area or seat count for the lounge, the lobby or any banquet room.
Matsuya has **47 rooms** (42 Japanese + 5 Japanese-Western) against the superseded hotel's 172,
so its lobby is a fraction of the size. Jalan's facility list does include `宴会場` and
conference rooms, and reviewers consistently report dinner and breakfast served in `個室`
private rooms, so the building can seat a party — it just may not hold 35 standing in the
lounge. **Action: agree the room with the hotel at check-in, with the banquet room or the 4F
dining floor as the named fallback.** Do not discover this at 19:30.

**Outdoor fallback changes too.** The old fallback was Fureai Bridge, 90 m from the old door.
From Matsuya, Fureai Bridge is ~1.25 km / ~18 min. The new near-field outdoor option is
**Takimi Bridge, ~290 m / ~5 min** — but it is a wooden, unlit, swaying suspension deck, so it
is a dawn frame, not a yukata-at-night frame.

## 2. The Day 4 dinner. **It is NOT a buffet. This hotel has no buffet at all.**

The itinerary prints *"18:30 Hotel Buffet."* Four independent channels say Matsuya serves
`和食会席` — kaiseki, plated to the table:

- yukoyuko: `和食会席料理`
- OZmall: `旬の食材をふんだんに使った和食会席`, in `大人な雰囲気のお食事処`
- JTB: dinner `和食会席`, breakfast `和定食`
- Rakuten: `お食事処にてご用意いたします`, breakfast `和食膳をご用意いたします`

Two stay reports confirm it in practice: dinner served at the table on the **4th floor** with
sukiyaki among the courses, breakfast likewise at the table rather than off a line, and
reviewers repeatedly mention `夕食も朝食も個室でゆっくりと時間を過ごすことができました` —
dinner and breakfast in **private rooms**. Nothing anywhere in Matsuya's published material
mentions a buffet, a live kitchen or a serving line.

**Sitting times: three, and they are CHOSEN, not assigned.**

> `午後6時からまたは午後6時30分から、午後7時から` — 18:00, 18:30 or 19:00.
> Rakuten independently prints the last seating as 19:00.
> Breakfast likewise: `午前7時からまたは午前7時30分から、午前8時から` — 07:00, 07:30 or 08:00.

This is the exact opposite of the superseded operator's arrangement, which was up to **six
sittings assigned at check-in** (`繁忙期の夕食開始時間は6部制となる場合があります。チェックイン
時にご案内します`). **That whole six-sitting analysis is void.** Matsuya has three published
start times and the guest picks one in advance.

**Is 10 minutes for 35 people into yukata any more achievable here?**
More achievable, but still not achievable. Two things improve: the yukata are in the room, so
there is no front-desk queue; and kaiseki actually suits a group *better* than a buffet —
everyone is seated and served simultaneously, nobody queues at a serving line, and a late table
does not mean cold leftovers. Two things do not: 18:20 → 18:30 is still ten minutes for lift
trips, rooms, changing and a group photograph in a 5-storey building where reception is on the
top floor; and a kaiseki service that has *started* cannot be drifted into the way a buffet can.

**Recommendation: take the 19:00 sitting, which the hotel openly offers.** That gives 40
minutes for keys → rooms → yukata → Mission 2 photo → dinner, and it is a request the hotel is
already set up to grant. If the agent insists on 18:30, the photo has to move to *after*
dinner — which the retired research already flagged as the safer plan at the old hotel too.

## 4. What the prefecture move does to Night 3

This is the biggest single change in the revision and it cuts both ways.

**What is lost.** The entire Iizaka/Anabara cluster is off the itinerary: Sabako-yu,
Totsuna-bashi's floodlit 1915 steel arch, Gyoza no Terui, Aberu Shoten's radium eggs, Tennoji
Anabara-yu, Kyu-Horikiri-tei, Harai-yu. None of it transfers. Night 3 is the only one of the
three where the surroundings had to be researched from zero.

**What is gained.** Three real things:

1. **The transfer.** Zao Fox Village → hotel drops from ~1 h 05 / ~45 km to **~30 min / ~18 km**, and check-in from 17:45 to 17:15. The brief's line that this hotel is "minutes from Zao Fox Village" is the point of the swap: the old routing crossed a prefecture border after a 16:40 finish at a site that already closes at 16:30.
2. **Group capacity.** 312 rooms, all ≥36 m², capacity ~800, 270 free parking spaces, a 268-seat restaurant, a dedicated inbound-group mailbox (`mercure.miyagizao.inbound@drhm.jp`). Yoshikawaya was a purpose-built group ryokan too, but this is an order of magnitude larger.
3. **Breakfast.** Locavore opens at **07:00** for an **08:00** departure. Yoshikawaya's house breakfast was 07:00 against the app's printed 07:30 — a half-hour mismatch that no longer exists.

**What is genuinely worse: there is nothing outside the building.** Yoshikawaya was isolated too
(Anabara is a six-ryokan pocket 2 km upstream of Iizaka), but it had Tennoji Anabara-yu 230 m
east and a shuttle to a real bath town. The Mercure has neither. Its own FAQ puts the nearest
convenience store at `車で約5分（徒歩約20分）` and a drugstore 15 minutes by car. Togatta
Onsen's bath street is ~2.6 km — beyond a 30-minute walk. Zao Rakuno Center's cheese factory,
the one landmark within a couple of kilometres, is **09:00–17:00**: shut before a 17:15 arrival
and shut long after an 08:00 departure, so the group can never reach it. There is no public
bath, no arcade, no lit street.

Consequence for the batch: **eleven of the twelve Zao `places` are on-property**, which is the
honest picture rather than a thin one. The single off-property record is the Seven-Eleven, and
it carries **no coordinate on purpose** — the FAQ does not name the branch, and neither
Seven-Eleven I could locate in Zao Town (遠刈田温泉仲町, 大字円田) is within 20 minutes' walk of
either candidate hotel position, so the real walk may be longer than the hotel's estimate.

**Practical knock-on:** buy water, snacks and an umbrella *before* leaving Zao Fox Village.
And the compensation is real — this is the darkest sky of the eight days, which is what the
`mustSee` courtyard record is for.

## 5. Findings that contradict or extend the existing batches

1. **`鬼怒川公園岩風呂` is permanently closed.** Nikko City's own page: `施設の老朽化による利用者
   の安全確保のため、令和6年3月31日（日曜日）をもって閉館しました`, and the Nikko tourism site
   now tags the spot 【閉館】. Its address was **日光市藤原19番地 — the same banchi as Matsuya.**
   So the nearest public bath to Night 4's door does not exist. Do not let anyone walk there.
2. **Takimi Bridge's leg was 17 minutes and is now ~5.** The retired record put it 1.3 km north
   of the old hotel; Matsuya *is* 1.3 km north. It changes from a coach stop on the way out to a
   genuine 05:45–06:30 walk. Conversely **Fureai Bridge goes from 2 minutes to ~18**, and the
   retired `mustSee` for it still carries a 2-minute leg — that record is now wrong for Night 4.
3. **Matsuya is by Kinugawa-**Koen** Station, not Kinugawa-Onsen.** `徒歩約３分`, 240 m; GSI's
   station record puts 鬼怒川公園駅 at 36.8415622, 139.7228694, ~122 m from the hotel's address
   point. The hotel has its own named bus stop, `花の宿松や前`, ten minutes by Tobu dial bus from
   Kinugawa-Onsen Station; a taxi between them is ~¥1,010. The whole Kinugawa-Onsen-Station-area
   cluster in the Day 4 batch is now ~1.1 km / ~15 min away, not walking-distance-trivial.
4. **Naruko needs no new town research at all.** Kounkaku is at `字車湯17`; the superseded Kameya
   was at `車湯54-6`; the GSI geocoder resolves **both to the identical point**
   (38.748074, 140.726532). onsen1.org independently gives Kounkaku as ~950 m / ~15 min from
   Naruko Onsen Station, matching Kameya's 0.95–1.2 km. Every leg in the retired Naruko cluster
   transfers unchanged. This batch therefore adds only on-property records plus one consolidated
   station-footbath record, and does not duplicate the town.
5. **Kounkaku's `百畳露天風呂` has closed** (JTB). Do not promise the hundred-mat open-air bath.
6. **Miyagi has a prefectural accommodation tax from 13 Jan 2026:** `1泊につき300円をフロントで
   申し受けます（大人・小人共）`, **on top of** bathing tax, collected in cash at the desk. This
   hits Nights 2 and 3 — two of the eight — and is not in the tour price. It is new since the
   existing batches were written and applies to every Miyagi stop.
7. **Mercure's tattoo policy is published in full and is the strictest on the trip:**
   `入れ墨、タトゥーを入れている方の大浴場のご入浴は原則お断りしております。ただし、8cm×10cmの
   カバーシール2枚を貼って隠せる場合のみご入浴いただけます` — two 8×10 cm stickers must actually
   cover it, sold at the front desk for ¥200. Kounkaku's is `大浴場利用不可（指定カバーシール有料
   で対応可）`. Matsuya publishes none.
8. **Mercure supplies a yukata but no towel to the bath.** `フリーサイズのお浴衣をお部屋にご用意
   しています`, XS/LL exchangeable at the desk, and `浴衣・スリッパでご利用いただけます` in the
   restaurant — so the group reads as one set in every indoor photo. But the bath answer is
   `お部屋からお持ちください`: carry your own towel down. (Yahoo Travel's amenity table lists
   `浴衣` among items *not* provided — that table is wrong; a guest review complains about the
   yukata's lack of pockets and its `変な上掛け` over-layer, so it exists.)
9. **Kounkaku's yukata location is genuinely unresolved.** JTB says `浴衣：客室に用意あり`;
   onsen1.org says they are chosen at the lobby in several sizes and patterns, which is the Ooedo
   house style. It only matters for the 18:30→19:00 turnaround, so settle it at check-in.
10. **Mercure's water is not source-fed.** `炭酸水素塩泉、塩化物泉、ナトリウム硫酸塩`, run
    `循環ろ過式、加水` — filtered and diluted. Gentler on silver than Naruko's sulphur, but do not
    sell it as a source-fed bath. The named open-air is `遠刈田温泉 不動の湯`, whose cypress tub
    *is* run `かけ流し`. Mercure rebranded from **Active Resorts Miyagi Zao in April 2024**.
11. **Matsuya's water is disputed:** `アルカリ性単純温泉` at pH 8.7, but Rakuten calls it
    `源泉かけ流し` while yukoyuko prints `配湯` — piped in. Bath names are `松の湯` and `滝見の湯`.
